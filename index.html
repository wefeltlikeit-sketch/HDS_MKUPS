PRE-DENOMINATOR STRATEGY — HEDIS MY 2026

The OMW Sequencing Problem

This is not a pool-size problem. The alert pool is large but stable — and the Stars team has accepted that. The real cost is acting on a fracture trigger before the full claims picture has arrived, then discovering afterward that the member was never truly a gap. The scoring layer doesn't change when Stars acts. It changes how confidently they act, and on whom.

How the Two Systems Currently Work Together

The rules engine and the Stars intervention workflow are separate systems with separate triggers. Understanding where each system's responsibility begins and ends is essential context for what the scoring layer adds.

Rules Engine

Continuous / Monthly
  • Evaluates all enrolled female members age 67–85
  • Applies exclusions: no prior fracture (90d), no BMD in 730d, no hospice, no advanced illness, no frailty, no OMW drug
  • Fires standing OMW-C alert for all who qualify
  • Alert pool is large but stable day-to-day
→

Stars Team

Real-Time / Event-Driven
  • Holds the engine's alert pool as their candidate universe
  • Watches their own ADT feed independently for a fracture event
  • Cross-references ADT fracture against alert pool
  • Launches intervention when both conditions are met
  • Prefers to control their own ADT-based activation
→

The Gap

Days to Weeks Later
  • BMD claim arrives with service date prior to the fracture
  • Member retroactively disqualified from the true OMW denominator
  • Intervention already completed — effort and cost already spent
  • Conversion cannot be counted — no real gap was closed
The sequencing mismatch: The ADT (non-claims data) arrives fast — Stars acts on it immediately. The BMD professional claim arrives later, but carries a service date that pre-dates the fracture. The clinical fact existed before the intervention. The administrative record did not. Acting on non-claims triggers without claims-speed exclusion coverage creates this gap.

The Cost Landscape

Each wasted intervention carries direct and indirect costs. The primary pain is measurable effort; secondary effects are plausible contributors worth investigating.

Primary Cost — Confirmed

Wasted intervention effort. Every outreach action has a cost — staff time, care manager capacity, outbound communication. Interventions on retroactively disqualified members produce no measurable Stars conversion and cannot be counted toward gap closure.

Secondary Cost — Plausible

Member & provider abrasion. A member who recently had a BMD or is actively managed for osteoporosis receives an outreach call about a gap that doesn't exist from their provider's perspective. Repeated misfires erode trust in the payer's clinical outreach.

Secondary Cost — Plausible

Alert fatigue. If the Stars team repeatedly finds that ADT-triggered interventions don't convert — because members self-resolve or retroactively disqualify — the team's confidence in the alert signal degrades over time. High-signal members get the same response cadence as low-signal ones.

What the Pre-Denominator Scoring Layer Solves

The scoring layer does not change the Stars team's activation workflow. It does not collapse the alert pool — that offer was made and Stars prefers their current model. What it does is attach predictive context to every member in the pool before the ADT fires, so that when Stars acts, they act with confidence rather than uncertainty.

Current State — When ADT Fires

Uniform Response to Every Alert

  • All ADT fracture cross-references produce the same alert weight
  • No signal about likelihood of self-resolution
  • No signal about whether this member has a BMD likely in the pipeline
  • No signal about whether this member will comply without intervention
  • Stars team learns the member didn't qualify days or weeks after acting
  • No mechanism to identify the genuinely addressable gap before acting
→
Proposed State — When ADT Fires

Scored, Ranked, Context-Rich

  • Pre-computed score already waiting in lookup table at ADT trigger time
  • DUAL_HIGH flag identifies members high on both fracture risk AND non-compliance risk
  • Members with any BMD in the prior 730 days are already excluded from the alert pool — only true gaps reach the scored population
  • Likely self-resolvers deprioritized before outreach resources are committed
  • Clinical signal summary in alert payload — team arrives with context, not a cold list
  • Highest-confidence gaps get same-day contact; low-confidence get a hold window
Design principle: Stars keeps their ADT activation workflow exactly as-is. The scoring engine enriches the alert pool they already hold — so that when their ADT fires, the response is differentiated, not uniform.

The Dual-Axis Scoring Engine

Every eligible member receives two independently computed sub-scores. The combination determines outreach priority — not a single composite number. A member can score low on fracture risk but high on non-compliance risk; the axes tell different stories.

FRAC Axis — Fracture Risk

Who is likely to fracture?

Signals: osteoporosis dx, prior fracture history, BMD T-score (lab/LOINC), corticosteroid use, aromatase inhibitors, falls history, BMI, age band.

Max meaningful score: ~17 pts — HIGH tier threshold ≥ 7

N-CMP Axis — Non-Compliance Risk

Who won't follow up if they do fracture?

Signals: no OMW drug ever, no DEXA ever, no osteoporosis dx, no PCP visit in 12 months, low medication PDC, no specialist visit in 24 months.

Max meaningful score: ~13 pts — HIGH tier threshold ≥ 5

The BMD Gap — and How to Close It

Drug exclusions arrive fast. BMD results don't. The asymmetry between these two data streams creates the retroactive-kick problem. This section maps the lag and proposes both tactical and strategic approaches — including new data sources the payer does not currently ingest.

The core asymmetry: Pharmacy claims for OMW-qualifying drugs arrive within 24–48 hours of dispensing — reliable real-time exclusion. BMD results travel through lab feeds and EMR supplemental workflows. The result already exists clinically, but the engine may not see it for days to weeks after the ADT fires.

Tactical: BMD History as a Compliance Signal (Corrected Framework)

An important constraint shapes how BMD history can be used as a scoring signal: any member with a BMD in the prior 730 days is already excluded from the alert pool entirely — they never reach the scored population. The 730-day lookback is an exclusion gate, not a tiering dimension.

This means every member who does appear in the scored pool either has no BMD on record at all, or has one older than 730 days. Within that population, history still tells two meaningfully different stories.

Key implication: The "self-resolver" risk — a member whose BMD arrives retroactively and disqualifies them after Stars intervenes — is not driven by members with BMDs in the 500–730 day range. Those members are already excluded. The retroactive disqualification risk comes from members whose BMD claim has a service date just before the fracture but whose claim arrives in the system after the ADT fires. This is a claims-lag problem, not a scoring-window problem.

BMD on Record — Older Than 730 Days

Prior Engagement — Lapsed

A provider has ordered a BMD at some point in this member's history — bone density is on their clinical radar. The test is simply too old to satisfy the OMW exclusion window. This member carries lower N-CMP risk than someone with no BMD ever: the care relationship around bone health exists. A new order is more likely than for a member with no history.

N-CMP signal: "No BMD ever" does NOT fire. Composite N-CMP score is lower. Outreach priority adjusted accordingly.

No BMD — Ever (Any Source)

True Gap — No Prior Engagement

No BMD found in claims, lab feeds, or EMR — across the member's full history. The provider has never ordered one. No care relationship around bone density screening exists. This member will not self-resolve after a fracture. Nothing is coming in the pipeline.

N-CMP signal: +3 pts fires. Combined with other non-compliance signals, this member reaches HIGH N-CMP tier rapidly. Highest outreach priority when ADT fracture fires.

Strategic: New Data Sources to Feed the Engine

These sources are not currently ingested. Each represents a different level of implementation effort and a different kind of signal — intent-to-test vs. result. Getting upstream of the result is the real prize.

🏥

The payer is integrating EPIC as its clinical workflow platform (UM/CM) by next year. EPIC surfaces pending orders and radiology appointments — not just completed results. A signed DEXA order in EPIC means a BMD result is days away, not weeks. This is the highest-value near-term signal for closing the BMD lag gap. Engine consumes via supplemental data workflow already established for HEDIS.

Note on Authorizations — Why BMDs Are a Gap Here
The engine currently supplements ADT records with inpatient prior authorization data — a reliable proxy when ADTs are missing, because inpatient services require auth. BMDs (outpatient DEXA) do not require prior authorization. ER visits similarly do not generate outpatient auths. This means UM authorization data cannot serve as a BMD proxy — there is nothing to look up. The EPIC scheduling feed and FHIR ServiceRequest are the correct substitutes for the "intent signal" that auth data provides for inpatient events.

Data Pipeline Architecture

The scoring engine runs monthly, pre-computes all scores, and stores them in a lookup table. When an ADT or real-time fracture signal fires, the enrichment layer retrieves the pre-computed score — no re-derivation at trigger time. Speed at the moment of outreach depends on work done upstream.

Event Flow

01
Monthly Score Run
Rules engine executes PRE_OMW_RISK_SCORE against all enrolled female members age 67+. Applies exclusion pre-filter. Computes FRAC and N-CMP sub-scores. Stores results in PRE_OMW_SCORE_TBL with run timestamp.
02
Real-Time Fracture Trigger
ADT event arrives with qualifying fracture diagnosis — OR inpatient authorization record with fracture code (used as ADT supplement when direct ADT is missing). ER fractures without auth are covered by direct ADT feed.
03
Score Lookup & Staleness Check
ADT enrichment layer queries PRE_OMW_SCORE_TBL on MBR_ID. Checks RUN_DT — if score is > 60 days old, flag as STALE and downgrade to MEDIUM priority. Stale records fall through to existing ADT workflow.
04
Exclusion Suppression Check
If EXCL_FLG = Y: suppress ADT outreach alert entirely. Member is pre-identified as non-addressable (hospice, active OMW drug, BMD within lookback, frailty, etc.). Alert does not reach the outreach queue.
05
Enriched Alert Delivery
Outreach queue receives alert payload: PRIORITY_TIER, DUAL_HIGH_FLG, COMPOSITE_SCORE, FRAC sub-score, N-CMP sub-score, and an auto-generated OUTREACH_NOTE summarizing the key signals that fired for this member.
06
Outreach Team Action
DUAL_HIGH and HIGH ★: same-day contact. MEDIUM: standard SLA. LOW: queue for follow-up only if capacity allows. Clinical note in alert means the care team arrives with context — not a cold call.

Backwards Compatibility

No Score Record Found

New enrollees or members not yet scored fall through to the existing ADT outreach workflow unchanged. The intelligence layer enriches — it does not replace — the current pipeline.

Score Exists — Exclusion Fired

If EXCL_FLG = Y, the ADT alert is suppressed before it reaches the outreach queue. This is the mechanism that prevents outreach to hospice members, members already on OMW drugs, and members with a recent BMD on record.

Phase 2 signal (PRIOR_NONCMPL): When a non-compliant denominator history table is built from prior measurement years, a +4 N-CMP signal fires for members who were previously in the OMW denominator and did not meet the numerator. This single signal will meaningfully sharpen score precision for the repeat-non-complier population.
